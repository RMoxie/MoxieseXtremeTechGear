PORTABLE POWER BUYING GUIDE

What size power bank do you actually need?

Compare capacity, charging speed, weight, and airline limits before choosing portable power for phones, tablets, or laptops.

Updated September 5, 2026

The best capacity is not always the largest one. A smaller pack is easier to carry; a larger pack provides more charging cycles but adds weight and can approach airline battery limits.

*Illustrative estimates use a common 3.7V nominal cell voltage. Use the watt-hour figure printed by the manufacturer for travel decisions. Conversion losses mean usable USB output will be lower than the stored nominal energy.

The airline rule that matters

FAA guidance classifies power banks as spare lithium-ion batteries. They belong in carry-on baggage. Packs rated from 0 to 100Wh are generally allowed; 101–160Wh require airline approval, and packs over 160Wh are forbidden on passenger aircraft. Airlines may apply stricter limits.

Before boarding: read the printed Wh label, protect the terminals from short circuit, and remove the pack if your carry-on bag is gate-checked.

Choose output as carefully as capacity

A high-capacity pack with a slow port can be frustrating. Phones may be satisfied with 20–30W, tablets and handhelds often benefit from 30–45W, and USB-C laptops commonly need 45–100W or more. Check the maximum single-port output and whether charging the bank while powering another device is supported.

Questions to ask before buying

  • Is the watt-hour rating clearly printed on the product?
  • Can one USB-C port deliver the wattage your laptop needs?
  • How long does the power bank itself take to recharge?
  • Does the included cable support the advertised output?
  • Is the weight reasonable for daily carry?

Frequently asked questions

Can a power bank go in checked luggage?

FAA guidance treats power banks as spare lithium batteries and requires them in carry-on baggage, not checked baggage.

How do I convert mAh to Wh?

Divide milliamp-hours by 1,000 to get amp-hours, then multiply by the battery’s nominal voltage: Wh = V × Ah.

Is a 27,000mAh power bank airline-safe?

Many models are designed to remain near 100Wh, but travelers should use the exact Wh rating printed by the manufacturer and check their airline’s rules.

Authoritative references: FAA PackSafe: lithium batteries and FAA airline passenger battery guidance.
