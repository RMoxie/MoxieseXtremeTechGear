🎮 Gaming Setup 101

New to building a setup? Start here. The four pillars in the right order, what monitor specs actually mean, why feel beats specs on mice and keyboards, and where spending more stops helping.

The Four Pillars, in Order

🔊 Sound First

You hear the game before you see it — footsteps, callouts, music. Audio is the upgrade you notice every single session.

🖱️ Hands Second

The mouse and keyboard are what you touch for hours. Comfort and reliability here beat every other spec on the box.

🖥️ Screen & Seat

The display you stare at and the chair you sit in. Both are long-session gear — buy for hours, not for photos.

📺 Content Last

Capture cards and stream decks turn play into content. They matter only after the playing itself feels great.

Monitor Specs, Translated

⚡ Refresh Rate

144Hz or higher is the single most noticeable display upgrade over 60Hz — motion goes from choppy to smooth. Past 240Hz, most players stop noticing.

🔍 Resolution

1080p with high refresh is the competitive pick; 1440p is sharper and prettier for story games. 4K demands a serious graphics card to keep up.

⏱️ Response Time

Lower means less ghosting behind moving objects. Treat 1ms claims as marketing-adjacent — panel type and real reviews matter more.

Mice & Keyboards: Feel Beats Specs

🎯 DPI Isn't Skill

800–1600 DPI is plenty for most players; polling rate (how often the mouse reports) matters more than a giant DPI number. Consistency beats extremes.

⌨️ Switches

Linear (smooth), tactile (a bump), clicky (a bump and a sound). There is no best — only what your fingers like after an hour, so try before you commit.

✋ Size & Grip

Palm, claw, or fingertip grip — and hand size — decide the right mouse shape more than any sensor spec. Weight matters too: lighter is easier to move all day.

Audio: Headset vs Mic + Headphones

🎧 Headset

The simplest path: one cable, mic included, designed for games. The right call for most players, and the easiest setup to live with.

🎙️ Separate Mic

A standalone mic sounds clearly better for streaming and voice work. Pair it with good headphones when your voice is part of the content.

🔓 Open vs Closed

Open-back headphones sound natural and airy but leak sound; closed-back blocks room noise and keeps game audio private. Roommates decide this one.

Comfort Is Performance

🪑 Chair vs Posture

A good chair helps, but monitor height, feet flat, and regular breaks matter more. No chair fixes a setup that has you hunching for hours.

💡 Lighting

A soft bias light behind the monitor reduces eye strain in dark rooms, and a lit room beats a pitch-black one for long sessions.

🧹 Cable & Desk Order

A tidy desk is a faster desk: cable routing, a monitor arm, and everything in reach. You play better when nothing is in the way.
