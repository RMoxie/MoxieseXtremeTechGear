🛍️ Black Friday 101

Black Friday is the biggest shopping day of the year — and the biggest stage for fake discounts. This guide teaches you to tell a real deal from a dressed-up regular price, so the money you spend goes to things actually worth it.

How a 'Deal' Gets Faked

🏷️ The Inflated Was-Price

The oldest trick: a price nobody actually paid becomes the 'was' price, so an ordinary price looks like a 40% cut. If the 'was' price seems high for the product, it probably never was the real price.

💲 List Price Isn't Street Price

Manufacturers set a list price, but good gear often sells below it all year. A deal measured against list price can still be worse than last month's everyday price. Compare against what it actually sold for, not the sticker.

⏰ The Countdown That Never Ends

Timers reset, 'almost gone' banners stay up for days, and 'today only' stretches into a week. Urgency is a sales tool. A real deal is still a deal tomorrow morning after you've slept on it.

The 5-Minute Check Before You Buy

📉 Price History

Look at what the item sold for over the past few months, not just today. If today's 'deal' matches its price from September, you are not saving anything — you are buying on schedule.

🏪 Who's Actually Selling

The same product from different sellers can mean different return policies, warranties, and shipping times. Know who you are buying from before you care about the price.

⭐ Reviews, Read Skeptically

A sudden flood of short, glowing reviews right before the holidays is a warning sign, not a recommendation. Read the three-star reviews first — they usually describe the real trade-offs.

↩️ Returns and Warranty

A deal with no return window or a 90-day warranty on something that should last years is not a deal. Check both before checkout, especially on big-ticket items.

Lightning Deals and Drop Culture

⚡ Scarcity Is the Product

'Only 3 left' and 'claimed 87%' are designed to make you buy before you think. Stock counters are sometimes real and sometimes theater. If you need it, buy it; if the counter is the reason, walk away.

🚶 When to Walk Away

The simplest test in this whole guide: if you would not buy it at full price, a discount does not make it a need. Black Friday's real cost is the stuff you bought because it was cheap.

When to Buy: Early, Day-Of, or Cyber Monday

🌅 Early Deals

Some of the best prices land weeks before Black Friday itself, especially on electronics. If the price is already at your target, waiting for the day itself only risks a sellout.

🛒 Black Friday Itself

The day is for doorbusters and the items that genuinely sell out — flagship gear, popular bundles, the things everyone wants at once. Have your list ready and check out fast.

💻 Cyber Monday

The online-focused categories get their second act here, and it is your second chance on anything you missed. Prices are often the same as Friday — the urgency is new, the deal usually is not.

After You Buy

👀 Watch for a Price Drop

Some stores adjust the price if it falls within a few days of your purchase. Keep the order email and check back — a two-minute message can refund the difference.

📦 Keep the Box

Returns need packaging, accessories, and receipts. Keep everything until you are sure the item is a keeper — holiday return windows close faster than you think.

🛡️ Register the Warranty

Big-ticket gear deserves the warranty registered on day one, while the purchase date is fresh. It takes five minutes and it is the difference between a repair and a replacement fight.
