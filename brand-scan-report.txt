A smaller pack is easier to carry; a larger pack provides more charging cycles but adds weight and can approach airline battery limits.</p><h2>Power-bank capacity comparison</h2><div class="comparison-wrap"><table class="comparison-table"><thead><tr><th>Rated capacity</th><th>Approximate nominal energy*</th><th>Best for</th><th>Recommended output</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>10,000mAh</td><td>About 37Wh</td><td>Daily phone carry and short outings</td><td>20–30W USB-C PD</td></tr><tr><td>20,000mAh</td><td>About 74Wh</td><td>Multi-day phone use, tablets, handhelds</td><td>45–65W when charging larger devices</td></tr><tr><td>About 27,000mAh</td><td>Near 100Wh</td><td>Laptop travel and maximum carry-on capacity</td><td>65–140W, matched to the laptop</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p><small>*Illustrative estimates use a common 3.7V nominal cell voltage. Conversion losses mean usable USB output will be lower than the stored nominal energy.</small></p><h2>The airline rule that matters</h2><p>FAA guidance classifies power banks as spare lithium-ion batteries. They belong in carry-on baggage. Packs rated from 0 to 100Wh are generally allowed; 101–160Wh require airline approval, and packs over 160Wh are forbidden on passenger aircraft. Airlines may apply stricter limits.</p><div class="decision-box"><strong>Before boarding:</strong> read the printed Wh label, protect the terminals from short circuit, and remove the pack if your carry-on bag is gate-checked.</div><h2>Choose output as carefully as capacity</h2><p>A high-capacity pack with a slow port can be frustrating. Phones may be satisfied with 20–30W, tablets and handhelds often benefit from 30–45W, and USB-C laptops commonly need 45–100W or more. Check the maximum single-port output and whether charging the bank while powering another device is supported.</p><h2>Questions to ask before buying</h2><ul><li>Is the watt-hour rating clearly printed on the product?</li><li>Can one USB-C port deliver the wattage your laptop needs?</li><li>How long does the power bank itself take to recharge?</li><li>Does the included cable support the advertised output?</li><li>Is the weight reasonable for daily carry?</li></ul><div class="article-cta"><a class="button primary" href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=airline+safe+USB-C+power+bank+100Wh&amp;tag=rmoxie-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Compare Travel Power Banks</a><a class="button secondary" href="/mobile-power.html">See Mobile Power Gear</a></div><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><section class="faq-item"><h3>Can a power bank go in checked luggage?</h3><p>FAA guidance treats power banks as spare lithium batteries and requires them in carry-on baggage, not checked baggage.</p></section><section class="faq-item"><h3>How do I convert mAh to Wh?</h3><p>Divide milliamp-hours by 1,000 to get amp-hours, then multiply by the battery’s nominal voltage: Wh = V × Ah.</p></section><section class="faq-item"><h3>Is a 27,000mAh power bank airline-safe?</h3><p>Many models are designed to remain near 100Wh, but travelers should use the exact Wh rating printed by the manufacturer and check their airline’s rules.</p></section><aside class="source-box"><strong>Authoritative references:</strong> <a href="https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/lithium-batteries" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FAA PackSafe: lithium batteries</a> and <a href="https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/airline-passengers-and-batteries" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FAA airline passenger battery guidance</a>.</aside></article></main>

The model, quantization level, context length, and whether other programs share the GPU determine what fits and how comfortably it runs.</p><h2>Quick answer: choose VRAM by the models you expect to use</h2><div class="comparison-wrap"><table class="comparison-table"><thead><tr><th>VRAM class</th><th>Practical starting point</th><th>Best for</th><th>Main limitation</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>8GB</td><td>Smaller quantized models and experimentation</td><td>Learning local AI on a budget</td><td>Limited model and context headroom</td></tr><tr><td>12GB</td><td>More flexible small-to-mid-size quantized models</td><td>General local assistants and coding tests</td><td>Large models still require compromises</td></tr><tr><td>16GB</td><td>Comfortable middle tier for broader model choices</td><td>Serious home use and development</td><td>High-end models may still spill into system memory</td></tr><tr><td>24GB+</td><td>More room for large quantized models and longer contexts</td><td>Advanced workflows and frequent experimentation</td><td>Higher cost, power, and cooling needs</td></tr></tbody></table></div><div class="decision-box"><strong>Important:</strong> these are planning tiers, not guarantees. A model file that barely fits can leave too little room for a useful context or smooth multitasking.</p><h2>NVIDIA, AMD, and integrated options</h2><ul><li><strong>NVIDIA:</strong> broad CUDA support makes compatibility straightforward for many local-AI tools, but compare VRAM—not only the GPU model name.</li><li><strong>AMD:</strong> ROCm support continues to expand, yet operating-system and GPU compatibility should be checked against the current framework documentation.</li><li><strong>Unified-memory systems:</strong> some integrated designs can dedicate a larger shared pool to AI workloads, but bandwidth, runtime support, and memory available to the operating system still matter.</li></ul><h2>Budget for the whole system</h2><p>Pair the GPU with enough system RAM, fast storage for model files, a suitable power supply, and airflow that can handle sustained workloads. wifi-7-vs-wifi-6e-guide.html:205:<main id="main-content"><section class="article-hero"><div class="article-hero-grid"><div><div class="breadcrumbs"><a href="/">Home</a> / Buying Guide</div><span class="eyebrow">HOME NETWORK BUYING GUIDE</span><h1>Wi-Fi 7 vs Wi-Fi 6E: which upgrade makes sense?</h1><p class="hero-copy">Understand 6GHz, 320MHz channels, Multi-Link Operation, mesh coverage, and device compatibility before replacing your router.</p><p class="content-updated"><time datetime="2026-09-05">Updated September 5, 2026</time></p></div><img class="article-hero-image" src="/wifi-guide.svg" alt="Illustration of a Wi-Fi router connecting a laptop, phone, and smart home" width="1200" height="675"></div></section><article class="article-body"><p>Wi-Fi 7 is the newest premium option, but a newer label does not automatically fix weak coverage. Floor plan, router placement, wired backhaul, device support, and internet speed usually matter as much as the wireless generation.</p><h2>Wi-Fi 6, 6E, and 7 compared</h2><div class="comparison-wrap"><table class="comparison-table"><thead><tr><th>Generation</th><th>Spectrum</th><th>Best fit</th><th>Main limitation</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Wi-Fi 6</td><td>2.4GHz and 5GHz</td><td>Strong value, broad device support</td><td>No 6GHz band</td></tr><tr><td>Wi-Fi 6E</td><td>2.4GHz, 5GHz, and 6GHz</td><td>Cleaner short-range spectrum for compatible devices</td><td>6GHz range is shorter and clients must support it</td></tr><tr><td>Wi-Fi 7</td><td>2.4GHz, 5GHz, and 6GHz</td><td>New multi-gig setups and demanding low-latency use</td><td>Higher cost and benefits require compatible clients</td></tr></tbody></table></div><h2>What Wi-Fi 7 actually adds</h2><p>Wi-Fi Alliance highlights 320MHz channels in the 6GHz band where local regulations permit them. It also highlights Multi-Link Operation, which lets compatible devices transmit and receive over multiple links to improve throughput, latency, and reliability.</p><div class="decision-box"><strong>Coverage reality:</strong> 6GHz offers additional clean spectrum but does not travel through walls as far as lower frequencies. Mesh node placement or wired Ethernet backhaul can matter more than the generation printed on the box.</div><h2>Who should upgrade now?</h2><ul><li><strong>Choose Wi-Fi 6</strong> for value, mature hardware, and homes with mostly older devices.</li><li><strong>Choose Wi-Fi 6E</strong> when several current devices support 6GHz and pricing is attractive.</li><li><strong>Choose Wi-Fi 7</strong> for new multi-gig internet, Wi-Fi 7 laptops or phones, low-latency local transfers, or a router expected to last several years.</li></ul><h2>Router or mesh?</h2><p>A centrally placed router can outperform an expensive mesh system in a compact home. Mesh is useful across multiple floors, long layouts, detached workspaces, or dense walls. Whenever possible, Ethernet backhaul gives mesh nodes a more dependable connection than wireless backhaul.</p><h2>Pre-purchase checklist</h2><ol><li>List the Wi-Fi generations your most important devices support.</li><li>Map weak-signal rooms and possible node locations.</li><li>Check whether the router’s Ethernet ports match your internet and local network speeds.</li><li>Confirm subscription requirements for security or parental-control features.</li><li>Keep firmware support and warranty length in the decision.</li></ol><div class="article-cta"><a class="button primary" href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Wi-Fi+7+router+mesh&amp;tag=rmoxie-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Compare Wi-Fi 7 Systems</a><a class="button secondary" href="/home-networking-security.html">See Networking &amp; Security Gear</a></div><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><section class="faq-item"><h3>Is Wi-Fi 7 faster than Wi-Fi 6E?</h3><p>Wi-Fi 7 can use features such as 320MHz channels and Multi-Link Operation, but real performance depends on compatible devices, local 6GHz availability, interference, distance, and the wired network.</p></section><section class="faq-item"><h3>Do I need Wi-Fi 7 for gigabit internet?</h3><p>No. A well-designed Wi-Fi 6 or 6E network can support many gigabit-class use cases. Wi-Fi 7 is most compelling for new high-end devices, low latency, multi-gig internet, or a long upgrade cycle.</p></section><section class="faq-item"><h3>Will Wi-Fi 7 improve old devices?</h3><p>Older devices can connect through backward compatibility, but they will not gain Wi-Fi 7 radio features. Better router placement and wired backhaul may still improve their experience.</p></section><aside class="source-box"><strong>Authoritative references:</strong> <a href="https://www.wi-fi.org/news-events/newsroom/wi-fi-alliance-introduces-wi-fi-certified-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wi-Fi Alliance: Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 7</a> and <a href="https://www.wi-fi.org/topic/6-ghz" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wi-Fi Alliance: 6GHz</a>.</aside></article></main>
