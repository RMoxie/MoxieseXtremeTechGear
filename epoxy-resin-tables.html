Epoxy & Resin for River Tables

Two different epoxies, two different jobs. The river itself needs a deep pour epoxy that can cure inches thick without cracking. The glossy skin on top needs a tabletop epoxy mixed thin and flooded on. Using tabletop resin for a 2-inch river (it overheats and cracks) — or deep pour for the finish coat (it stays soft too long) — is the #1 beginner mistake. Before You Pour: The 5 Rules of a Clean River Table

1. Seal coat first. Brush a thin coat of tabletop epoxy on the live edges and let it tack up before the deep pour — it seals the wood pores that would otherwise pump bubbles into your river for hours. 2. Respect the ratio. Deep pours are usually 2:1, tabletop 1:1, and they are not interchangeable; measure by volume, mix a full two minutes, scrape the sides. 3. Pour in a warm room. 70–80°F keeps viscosity low and bubbles rising; cold resin traps air and can cure cloudy. 4. Torch, don't stir. A quick pass with a heat gun or torch 10 minutes after pouring pops surface bubbles — keep it moving so you don't scorch the resin. 5. Patience is an ingredient. Deep pours want 3–7 days before demolding and sanding. Rush it and you'll dent a surface that would have cured rock hard.
